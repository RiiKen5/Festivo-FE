# Ideal Customer Profiles

Behaviour-first profiles: what makes each group look for a solution, where they look, and what stops them. Start in **one city (Pune)**, where launch vendors will be onboarded.

## ICP 1 — Home & micro vendors (SUPPLY, highest priority)

**Who:** Home bakers, balloon/floral decorators, freelance event photographers, DJs, small caterers, terrace/rooftop venue owners. 1–5 person businesses.

| | |
|---|---|
| Use case | Take and manage event orders; get paid reliably |
| Problem | Orders scattered across Instagram DMs and WhatsApp; chasing advances; no-shows and late cancellations; no way to prove reliability to new customers |
| Trigger | A customer ghosts after a quote; an advance dispute; wanting more orders in slow months |
| Motivation | Predictable income, fewer wasted quotes, looking professional |
| Objections | "15% is too much", "my customers are on Instagram", "another app to check", "when do I get paid?" |
| Discovery behaviour | Instagram (their main storefront), WhatsApp groups of home bakers/decorators, Facebook groups, YouTube tutorials, supplier shops (cake-supply and balloon wholesale stores) |
| Alternatives | Instagram + WhatsApp + UPI (free), JustDial/Sulekha paid leads, Urban Company-style platforms (rarely for events) |
| Willingness to pay | Proven for paid leads; **commission on own customers is unproven**. They will pay for *new* customers more readily than for customers they bring. |

**What wins them:** "Customers pay before the event, you get paid after it, no chasing." Plus free listing, and visible new demand.

## ICP 2 — Celebration organizers (DEMAND)

**Who:** Urban 22–40 year-olds planning a birthday, anniversary, baby shower or house party for 15–60 people; parents planning kids' birthdays; friends organizing a surprise.

| | |
|---|---|
| Use case | Book 1–3 vendors (cake, decor, photographer/DJ) for a dated event |
| Problem | Don't know whom to trust; prices hidden in DMs; risky advances |
| Trigger | An upcoming date (1–4 weeks out). **Search spikes are date-driven.** |
| Motivation (functional) | Get it done without risk. (Emotional) Look good to guests, no day-of disasters. (Social) "I threw a great party." |
| Objections | "Is this cheaper than DMing directly?", "What if the vendor isn't good?", "Do I have to create an account?" |
| Discovery | Google ("balloon decoration at home pune", "custom cake near me"), Instagram reels, friends' recommendations, society WhatsApp groups |
| Frequency | 2–4 events/year, so acquisition must be cheap or come through loops |

**Highest-value sub-segment:** parents of young children (kids' birthdays every year, several vendors per event, high trust anxiety).

## ICP 3 — Community & small-event hosts (DEMAND + LOOP)

**Who:** Meetup organizers, housing-society committees, small venue owners hosting ticketed nights, college clubs, corporate office admins planning farewells.

| | |
|---|---|
| Use case | Invite people, collect RSVPs or sell tickets, check people in |
| Problem | Google Forms + UPI screenshots + WhatsApp lists |
| Trigger | A recurring event |
| Discovery | Peer recommendation, Instagram, "free ticketing for events" search |
| Alternatives | Luma, Townscript, AllEvents, KonfHub, Google Forms |
| Value to Festivo | **Each event exposes 20–200 guests** to Festivo; recurring hosts retain |

## ICP 4 — Guests (LOOP, not a target for spend)

People who RSVP or buy a ticket. Never market to them directly; convert them with an in-context "Planning your own event?" path.

## Priority

1. **Vendors (ICP 1)**: supply first; vendors also bring their existing customers.
2. **Recurring hosts (ICP 3)**: cheap, loop-driven exposure.
3. **Organizers (ICP 2)**: once there are ≥15 good vendors per launch category; via search and vendor-led sharing.
